# GHL MARKET SIGNALS BOARD — OPERATING DOCUMENTATION

## 1. Purpose

The GHL Market Signals Board exists to turn market reality into action.

It is not a database of random job posts.

It is a decision tool that helps answer:

* What are buyers already paying for?
* Which problems repeat?
* Which problems are close to money, leads, sales, time, risk, or operational bottlenecks?
* Which GHL skills should I learn first?
* Which proof should I build?
* Which offer should I test?
* Which tasks should enter the GHL / Money execution flow?

The board should support this loop:

```text
Market signal
→ paid problem
→ selected opportunity
→ proof to build
→ offer
→ proposal / outreach
→ paid task
→ case / template / insight
→ stronger next cycle
```

The board exists to prevent random learning, abstract planning, and tool exploration without buyer, pain, proof, offer, or distribution.

---

## 2. Core Principle

The board is successful only if it creates better actions.

A good Market Signal Board should produce:

* better GHL learning priorities;
* better freelance service ideas;
* better proof/case decisions;
* better Upwork proposals;
* better website/profile positioning;
* better outreach messages;
* better delivery templates;
* better understanding of buyer pain;
* better chances of getting paid.

If the board becomes a beautiful archive but does not generate action, it is failing.

---

## 3. Main Question

The board should not ask:

```text
Is this interesting?
```

It should ask:

```text
Is there a real buyer with pain, budget, urgency, and a reason to pay now?
```

A signal is valuable when it reveals:

* who the buyer is;
* what they are trying to fix;
* what business outcome is blocked;
* what they are willing to pay for;
* what skills are required;
* what proof would reduce their risk;
* how this could become an offer.

### Difficulty

How hard it would be for you to deliver.

Recommended values:

```text
1 = very easy
2 = easy
3 = medium
4 = hard
5 = too hard for now
```

Difficulty should include:

* technical complexity;
* unclear requirements;
* client communication risk;
* tool access;
* testing difficulty;
* responsibility level.

---

### Speed to Cash

How quickly this could turn into money.

Recommended values:

```text
1 = slow / unclear
2 = possible but weak
3 = moderate
4 = fast
5 = very fast
```

High speed to cash usually means:

* buyer already knows the problem;
* scope is small;
* outcome is clear;
* proof can be built quickly;
* delivery can be done in days, not months;
* buyer likely has budget.

---

### Opportunity Score

Overall score from 1 to 5.

Use this formula:

```text
Opportunity Score =
Buyer + Pain + Budget + Urgency + Repeatability + Proof Potential + Speed to Cash
```

Simple scoring:

```text
1 = weak signal
2 = interesting but unclear
3 = valid signal
4 = strong opportunity
5 = priority opportunity
```

A score of 4–5 means:

* real buyer;
* painful problem;
* budget signal;
* urgency;
* repeated pattern;
* possible offer;
* proof can be built;
* realistic path to proposal or paid work.

## 9. What Counts As A Real Market Signal

A signal is valid if it shows at least one of:

* buyer;
* problem;
* pain;
* budget;
* urgency;
* required skill;
* repeated demand;
* exact buyer language;
* possible offer;
* proof opportunity.

Strong signal:

```text
A marketing agency is hiring someone to fix GHL workflows because leads from Facebook Ads are not entering the correct pipeline, and they need it fixed this week.
```

Weak signal:

```text
Someone mentions GHL is useful.
```

---

## 10. What Not To Add

Do not add:

* random tutorials;
* generic GHL feature ideas;
* vague business ideas;
* abstract trends;
* content with no buyer;
* tools without pain;
* ideas with no budget signal;
* tasks that are interesting but not connected to money, proof, offer, or distribution.

If it does not help choose what to learn, build, sell, or test, it does not belong in this board.

## 14. Collection Rules

When collecting signals:

1. Do not rewrite buyer language too much.
2. Capture the exact phrase when possible.
3. Do not judge too early.
4. Do not turn every signal into a task.
5. Do not choose an offer from one signal.
6. Look for repeated pain.
7. Separate task from business pain.
8. Separate interesting from payable.
9. Mark unclear fields as “Unknown”.
10. Move quickly.

## 15. Signal Processing Workflow

### Step 1 — Capture

Add raw signals quickly.

Fill only:

```text
Date
Source
Link
Buyer Type
Problem
Exact Buyer Language
Budget Signal
Urgency Signal
Required Skills
Status = Raw Signal
```

Do not overthink.

---

### Step 2 — Clarify

After collecting a batch, fill:

```text
Business Pain
Related Tools
Possible Offer
Proof I Could Build
Difficulty
Speed to Cash
```

---

### Step 3 — Pattern Detection

Look across rows and mark:

```text
Repeated Pattern = Yes / Maybe / No
```

Questions:

```text
Have I seen this problem before?
Does the same buyer type ask for it?
Does the same pain repeat?
Does the same GHL skill appear?
Does this look like a service package?
```

---

### Step 4 — Score

Give Opportunity Score 1–5.

Score high if:

* buyer is clear;
* pain is expensive;
* budget is visible;
* urgency is strong;
* pattern repeats;
* delivery is realistic;
* proof can be built;
* offer is simple to explain.

---

### Step 5 — Select

Choose one opportunity.

Selection rule:

```text
Pick one opportunity that is repeated, close to cashflow, realistic to deliver, and easy to prove.
```

Do not choose 5 opportunities at once.

---

### Step 6 — Convert To Action

Create execution tasks:

```text
Build proof for selected opportunity
Write offer
Write proposal snippet
Send 3 proposals
Create delivery checklist
Update profile if needed
```

The board is only useful when it produces action.

## 16. Opportunity Selection Criteria

Use this checklist before selecting an opportunity:

```text
Is there a real buyer?
Is there visible pain?
Is the problem close to revenue, leads, sales, time saved, or risk reduced?
Is there a budget signal?
Is there urgency?
Does this repeat across multiple signals?
Can I build proof in 1–3 days?
Can I explain the offer in one sentence?
Can I deliver a small version?
Can this become a template or checklist?
Can this lead to paid work soon?
```

Select only if most answers are “yes”.

## 22. How To Turn Signals Into Proposals

Proposal should use:

```text
Exact Buyer Language
Business Pain
Proof
Simple Next Step
```

Proposal structure:

```text
1. Acknowledge the specific problem.
2. Show that you understand the business pain.
3. Mention relevant proof or similar setup.
4. Ask one clear diagnostic question.
5. Suggest a simple next step.
```

Example:

```text
Hi [Name], I saw you need help fixing your GHL workflow so new leads go into the right pipeline and get proper follow-up.

This usually becomes a real sales problem because leads can be missed or contacted too late. I can help check the current flow, fix the trigger/pipeline logic, and test the full path from form submission to follow-up.

A quick question: are the leads coming from a GHL form, Facebook Lead Ads, or another source?

I can start by reviewing the current workflow and mapping what needs to be fixed.
```

```text
If proposals are weak → improve Exact Buyer Language and Business Pain fields.
If delivery is slow → link selected signals to Delivery Asset Library.
If too much noise accumulates → improve archive rules.
```
